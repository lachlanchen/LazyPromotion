When a knowledge graph says two ideas are related, the link is only as useful as its evidence. A good graph keeps three things together: the claim, the exact passage that supports it, and a fingerprint of the source file. Translation adds another layer. The original phrase, the translated phrase, and the translator's uncertainty should stay visible side by side. This makes correction easier. A reader can follow the link back to the passage, check the wording, and decide whether the relationship still holds. Provenance does not make every claim true, but it makes each claim inspectable.
